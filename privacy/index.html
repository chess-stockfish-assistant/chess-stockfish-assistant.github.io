Chess Assistant - Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 29, 2026

Overview

Chess Assistant is a chess study and analysis tool designed to help players learn, improve their skills, and understand chess positions. All chess analysis runs on your device using the open-source Stockfish engine. We are committed to protecting your privacy. This policy explains how the app works and what data is handled.

Purpose

Chess Assistant is intended as an educational and training tool for chess players who want to:

  • Study and analyze chess positions in real time
  • Learn from move classifications (Brilliant, Great, Best, Inaccuracy, Mistake, Blunder)
  • Explore different play styles (Aggressive, Defensive, Positional, etc.)
  • Improve their understanding of openings, middlegames, and endgames

This app is not designed, intended, or promoted for use as a cheating tool in competitive or rated games. Users are solely responsible for how they use the app and must comply with the terms of service of any chess platform they use.

Data Collection

We collect as little data as possible. The only information that leaves your device is the free-tier usage counter, the usage statistics and, only if you agree to them, the board-reading reports described below. All of them use a pseudonymous device identifier. Nothing else is collected.

  • All chess analysis is performed locally on your device using the Stockfish engine.
  • No board positions, moves, or game data are sent to any server, except in the optional board-reading reports described below.
  • No advertising data is collected, and you are not tracked across other apps or websites.
  • No account registration is required (usernames are stored locally on your device only).

Free-Tier Usage Counter

The free version allows a limited number of analyses per day. To enforce this limit fairly, the free version sends the following to our server (operated by the developer) over an encrypted HTTPS connection:

  • A one-way hash (SHA-256) of the device identifier that Android assigns to this app (Android ID). The hash cannot be turned back into the identifier and is not linked to your name, email, Google account, or any other information.
  • A random installation ID generated by the app.
  • The number of free analyses used.
  • The version of the app.

To confirm that it is the genuine app installed from Google Play and running on a genuine Android device, the free version also requests an integrity check from Google Play (Google Play Integrity API). Google performs this check under its own privacy policy; our server receives only the result and stores whether the check passed and, when it did not, a short code for the reason (for example, that the app was not installed from Google Play). If you have a free trial of the premium features, the server also keeps the date it ends, and may end it early when the check fails.

This information is used only to count free analyses per day and to prevent abuse of the free tier. It is not sold, shared with third parties, or used for advertising. To limit abuse, the server keeps your IP address in memory for at most one hour; it is not stored with the counter records, the usage statistics or the board-reading reports. Counter records are deleted automatically 30 days after the app last reports. After that, the server keeps only the one-way hash with the date it was first seen and the date its free trial ended, so that reinstalling the app does not start a new trial; this is deleted 400 days after the app last reported. Premium subscribers do not send this information. Because a record is identified only by the one-way hash, we cannot find it from your name, email address or any other information about you.

Usage Statistics

To understand how the app is used and where people run into trouble (for example, when enabling the accessibility service), all versions of the app, free and premium, send usage statistics to the same server over HTTPS:

  • Which steps were used: opening the app, the accessibility, overlay and notification permission prompts (and your answers), starting and stopping the assistant, the premium offer and free-trial screens that were shown (with how many analyses the trial used), premium upgrade and purchase attempts, the Google Play rating card, the notification that free analyses are available again, and your answer to the board-reading reports question.
  • The settings in use: platform (Chess.com or Lichess), play style, depth and the on/off options.
  • Whether you have Premium or a free trial running.
  • How long each session lasted, how many suggestions and analyses it had, whether a chess board was detected, and when a daily limit or pause was reached.
  • How well the board was read in each session: how many positions were read, how often whose turn it was had to be guessed, how many incomplete boards were skipped, how often the Lichess move list was scrolled, whether the Lichess colour was detected, guessed or set by you, and why a board could not be read (for example, a missing move list). These are counts and short codes, never the content of the screen.
  • The app version, the Android version, the device manufacturer and model, the app language and the country. The country comes from the SIM card, the mobile network or the language setting; the app does not use location permissions or GPS.
  • A security status of the app, such as whether it was installed from Google Play.

These statistics are sent with the same one-way device hash and installation ID described above. They never include board positions, moves, usernames, or any content read by the accessibility service. They are used only to improve the app and to prevent abuse, are not sold or shared with third parties, and are deleted after 90 days.

Board-Reading Reports (Optional)

Only if you agree (the app asks first, and you can change your answer at any time in the assistant's settings, ☰), the app sends a board-reading report when it cannot read the board of a game on screen, when the chess app has been on screen for 30 seconds without a board being read, when whose turn it is seems wrong, when a move does not fit the position, or when you tap “Report” in the assistant's settings (the app then asks what went wrong, for example “Tournament or another game mode”, and sends your choice). A report contains what the accessibility service saw of the chess app's screen, limited to:

  • The position and size of the elements on the screen, their type (for example, a button or a text), and the names the chess app gives to its screens and elements (for example “board_view”), which do not identify you.
  • Only the texts that describe the game: moves and move numbers, piece and square descriptions (including the labels of board squares the app cannot read yet), and clocks. Every other text, such as player names, ratings, chat messages and menus, is removed on your device, and only its length is sent.
  • What the app made of the game: the position it reconstructed (and, when a move did not fit, the position before it), whose turn it thinks it is, which side it thinks you play, where it placed the board, and its last suggested move.
  • The chess app and its version, the screen size, the app language, the app version, and the same one-way device hash and installation ID described above.

Reports are used only to fix board reading. They are not sold or shared with third parties, and are deleted after 14 days. At most five automatic reports are sent per day.

Accessibility Service

Chess Assistant uses Android's Accessibility Service only when the user explicitly enables the automatic board-reading feature.

The Accessibility Service is used to identify and reconstruct chess positions displayed in supported chess applications, including Chess.com and Lichess. To perform this function, the service may read accessibility elements related to the chess game, such as piece positions, board squares, moves, move numbers, clocks, and information necessary to determine the current position and side to move.

This information is processed locally on the user's device to provide chess study and analysis features using the Stockfish engine.

The Accessibility Service is not used to read passwords, private messages, conversations, contacts, payment information, or unrelated content from other applications.

Information obtained through the Accessibility Service is not transmitted to our servers except when the user has separately opted in to the optional Board-Reading Reports described in this Privacy Policy.

When enabled, Board-Reading Reports may contain limited chess-game information required to diagnose board-reading problems. These reports are used only for troubleshooting board-reading functionality and are automatically deleted after 14 days.

Users can disable the Accessibility Service at any time through Android settings. Disabling it prevents automatic board reading but does not prevent access to other features that do not require the service.

In-App Purchases

Premium features are available via Google Play In-App Purchases. All payment processing is handled entirely by Google Play. We do not have access to your payment information.

Data Storage

The app stores the following data locally on your device using Android SharedPreferences:

  • Your chosen username (for display purposes only)
  • App settings (depth, style mode, preferences)
  • Premium subscription status (synced with Google Play)
  • Free-tier usage counters

Apart from the usage counter and the usage statistics described above (which include the settings in use, never the username), this data never leaves your device and is deleted when you uninstall the app.

Third-Party Services

The app uses the following third-party services:

  • Google Play Billing — for processing in-app purchases. Subject to Google's Privacy Policy.
  • Google Play Integrity API — free version only, to verify the app and the device as described above. Subject to Google's Privacy Policy.

The usage counter server is operated by the developer and is not a third-party service.
